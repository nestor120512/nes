**Title:** Splenic infarction as a manifestation of antiphospholipid syndrome with isolated IgM antibodies in late-onset lupus: a case report

**Topic:** Systemic Lupus Erythematosus / Antiphospholipid Syndrome

**Keywords:** Autoantibodies · Imaging · Descriptive Studies

---

### Antecedentes/Objetivo – Background/Objective  (494 caracteres)

The 2023 ACR/EULAR classification criteria for antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) weight antiphospholipid antibodies (aPL) by isotype: isolated IgM anticardiolipin (aCL) and anti-β2-glycoprotein I (aβ2GPI) antibodies contribute only 1 of the 3 required laboratory points, whereas the Sydney criteria accept them. Objective: To describe splenic infarction in late-onset systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) with isolated IgM aPL, classifiable as APS by the Sydney but not the 2023 ACR/EULAR criteria.

### Métodos – Methods  (179 caracteres)

Retrospective medical record review. Clinical manifestations, aPL profile, imaging studies, etiological workup of the infarction, therapeutic decisions, and outcome were analyzed.

### Resultados – Results  (1,590 caracteres)

A 63-year-old woman whose SLE presented with pancytopenia in July 2026 was treated with methylprednisolone pulses, prednisolone, and azathioprine. At baseline, she had IgM double positivity: aCL 69.38 U/mL (positive ≥12) and aβ2GPI >200 RU/mL (positive ≥20), with negative IgG aCL, IgG aβ2GPI, and lupus anticoagulant. Two months later, she was readmitted with pancytopenia (hemoglobin 8.6 g/dL; platelets 46,000/µL; no schistocytes), positive direct Coombs test, livedo reticularis, serositis with ascites, nephrotic-range proteinuria (3.8 g/24 h), and marked hypocomplementemia (C3 52 mg/dL; C4 <2.9 mg/dL). Abdominal computed tomography showed a peripheral wedge-shaped lesion in the lower pole of the spleen, consistent with splenic infarction (Figure 1); Doppler ultrasound showed no portal vein thrombosis or portal hypertension, and electrocardiogram and echocardiogram were normal. At 12 weeks, IgM double positivity persisted (aCL 80 U/mL; aβ2GPI high-positive), with lupus anticoagulant again negative. She met the Sydney but not the 2023 ACR/EULAR criteria, scoring only 1 laboratory point. Nevertheless, APS secondary to SLE was considered on clinical grounds. Nephrotic-range proteinuria may have contributed as a prothrombotic factor. She received further methylprednisolone pulses, hydroxychloroquine, and mycophenolate mofetil for presumed lupus nephritis (the patient declined biopsy). Once platelets reached 127,000/µL, she was anticoagulated with enoxaparin 1 mg/kg as a bridge to warfarin (target INR 2-3). At 2 weeks, platelets were 239,000/µL and hemoglobin 10.0 g/dL.

### Conclusión – Conclusion  (436 caracteres)

This case illustrates the discordance between both systems: an isolated, persistent, high-titer IgM profile with thrombosis at an unusual site in the setting of SLE meets the Sydney but not the 2023 ACR/EULAR criteria, which trade sensitivity for specificity. Classification criteria do not replace clinical judgment: therapeutic decisions should integrate the thrombotic event, aPL profile, alternative risk factors, and bleeding risk.
