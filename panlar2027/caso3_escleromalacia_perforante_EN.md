**Title:** Severe scleromalacia perforans without identifiable systemic autoimmune disease: a therapeutic dilemma for the rheumatologist. A case report

**Topic:** Mixed (Miscellaneous Diseases)

**Keywords:** Rare/orphan diseases · Glucocorticoids · Descriptive Studies

---

### Antecedentes/Objetivo – Background/Objective  (548 caracteres)

Scleromalacia perforans is a necrotizing anterior scleritis without inflammation, classically associated with rheumatoid arthritis and described in granulomatosis with polyangiitis, relapsing polychondritis, and Behçet's disease. Without an identifiable systemic disease, the rheumatologist faces a dilemma: whether to immunosuppress without a diagnosis or classification criteria when an organ is at risk. Objective: To describe this dilemma in an older woman with severe scleromalacia perforans, in whom ophthalmology requested immunosuppression.

### Métodos – Methods  (179 caracteres)

Retrospective review of the medical records. Systemic manifestations, immunological profile, infectious evaluation, multidisciplinary decisions, and ocular outcomes were analyzed.

### Resultados – Results  (1,508 caracteres)

A 71-year-old woman with no known autoimmune disease, ocular surgery, or trauma had painless right conjunctival hemorrhage since October 2024 and a temporal scleral defect since February 2025; after loss to follow-up, she was admitted in June 2026 with count-fingers visual acuity and extensive corneoscleral destruction with uveal and iris herniation (Figure 1). Targeted rheumatologic evaluation found no arthritis, ulcers, or ear, nose, and throat involvement. Rheumatoid factor was borderline (14.8) with negative anti-CCP, and ANA, anti-dsDNA, extractable nuclear antigen panel, anticentromere, and PR3- and MPO-ANCA were negative, with normal complement. Infectious disease evaluation, including a negative tuberculin skin test, normal chest CT, and negative serologies for syphilis and hepatitis B and C, identified no infectious etiology. Without a systemic diagnosis, the indication was based on the threat to the organ: she received intravenous methylprednisolone 500 mg/day for 5 days, followed by tapering oral prednisone and azathioprine as a steroid-sparing agent, given her age and risk of toxicity. The ophthalmology board estimated involvement of 60-70% of the ocular wall, and a joint decision with rheumatology was made to continue immunosuppression to preserve the globe and protect the left eye. The globe remained unperforated until tectonic corneoscleral patch grafting in November; the left eye, which underwent cataract surgery in October under immunosuppression, remains unaffected.

### Conclusión – Conclusion  (454 caracteres)

Severe scleromalacia perforans may occur without an identifiable systemic autoimmune disease, and the absence of classification criteria does not exclude an immune-mediated ocular process. This case illustrates the need to individualize immunosuppression according to ocular severity, through shared decision-making between ophthalmology and rheumatology, reasonable exclusion of infection, and follow-up to detect emerging systemic disease and toxicity.
