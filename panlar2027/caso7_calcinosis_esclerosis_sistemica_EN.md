# Case 7 – Calcinosis in systemic sclerosis (final English version for the PANLAR 2027 form)

**Title:** What the skin hides: two clinical-radiological extremes of calcinosis in systemic sclerosis

**Topic:** 7. Imaging in Rheumatology

**Keywords:** Imaging · Skin · Descriptive Studies

### Background/Objective

Calcinosis cutis is common in systemic sclerosis (SSc) and causes pain, ulceration, infection and disability; no treatment has proven effective in controlled clinical trials. It is associated with the limited cutaneous subset, anticentromere antibodies, digital ulcers and acro-osteolysis, supporting a role for chronic ischemia. Its burden is often underestimated on physical examination. Objective: to illustrate, through clinical-radiological correlation, the two extremes of its spectrum: the acral and the massive soft-tissue forms.

### Methods

Retrospective review of the medical records, clinical photographs and plain radiographs of two patients with limited cutaneous SSc and calcinosis, correlating clinical and radiological findings.

### Results

In both patients, serum calcium, phosphate and creatinine were normal, supporting dystrophic calcinosis. Patient 1: a 48-year-old woman with limited cutaneous SSc (skin induration of the hands, sclerodactyly and expressionless facies), ANA 1:640 with centromere pattern, anticentromere-positive and anti-Scl-70-negative. Pelvic radiography revealed massive, bilateral and symmetric calcinosis, with confluent plaque-like deposits and tumor-like conglomerates in the gluteal and trochanteric regions and the lateral thighs (Figure 1A), far more extensive than the visible lesions suggested. Clinically, it presented as painful subcutaneous nodules on the flank and buttocks, several ulcerated and draining chalky white material, with scars from previous surgical removals (Figure 1B). Patient 2: a 41-year-old woman with limited cutaneous SSc (skin induration of the face and hands), Raynaud's phenomenon, prior digital ulcers, microstomia and Sjögren's syndrome. She had clustered punctate white deposits on several fingertips, with flexion contractures (Figure 2A). Hand radiographs showed multiple bilateral soft-tissue calcifications in the fingertips, most prominent in the left thumb and both middle fingers, coexisting with acro-osteolysis (Figure 2B).

### Conclusion

Calcinosis in SSc is not a single entity: it ranges from punctate acral deposits, associated with digital vasculopathy, to massive proximal conglomerates that may occur even in limited cutaneous disease, far from thickened skin. The skin shows only a fraction; plain radiography, widely available and inexpensive, reveals its true extent and associated lesions such as acro-osteolysis. In patients with SSc and painful or ulcerated nodules, particularly those with anticentromere antibodies or digital ulcers, targeted radiographs should be considered.
