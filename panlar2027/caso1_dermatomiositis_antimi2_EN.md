**Title:**
Polycyclic anti-Mi-2 juvenile dermatomyositis with repeated response to intravenous immunoglobulin: a case report

**Topic:** Inflammatory Myopathies (Miopatías Inflamatorias)

**Keywords:** Descriptive Studies · Autoantibodies · Magnetic Resonance Imaging

---

### Antecedentes/Objetivo – Background/Objective  (537 caracteres)

Anti-Mi-2-positive juvenile dermatomyositis is typically associated with prominent muscle involvement, a low risk of interstitial lung disease, and a good response to immunosuppression, and is therefore considered to have a favorable prognosis. However, some patients follow a polycyclic course, with relapses that require treatment escalation. Objective: To describe a patient with polycyclic anti-Mi-2-positive juvenile dermatomyositis and repeated improvement after the addition of intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) as rescue therapy.

### Métodos – Methods  (134 caracteres)

Retrospective case report based on review of medical records, laboratory studies, muscle biopsy, and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI).

### Resultados – Results  (1,635 caracteres)

A female patient presented at age 17 with Gottron's sign and other skin lesions, myalgia, and progressive proximal weakness. One month later, she had proximal strength of 3/5, dysphagia, creatine kinase (CK) of 12,588 U/L, ANA 1:160, and positive anti-Mi-2α/β antibodies; muscle biopsy showed inflammatory myopathy (2017 EULAR/ACR score: 9.6). She received methylprednisolone pulses, prednisolone, and azathioprine; for persistent weakness and dysphagia, IVIG 2 g/kg was added, with functional improvement and recovery of swallowing. During follow-up, lupus overlap was considered given ANA 1:1280 and facial erythema, but reassessment did not support it (negative anti-dsDNA and anti-RNP, normal complement, no nephritis). Over the next 19 months, she had four relapses after periods of complete response (Table 1): mycophenolate was added after the first, the second occurred after its discontinuation, and the third during glucocorticoid tapering. At the fourth relapse (age 19), she had strength of 2/5, inability to walk, and CK of 5,720 U/L. Despite cushingoid features, elevated CK and diffuse muscle edema with fascial involvement on thigh MRI pointed to inflammatory activity rather than steroid-induced myopathy. After 5 days of methylprednisolone, CK fell to 2,073 U/L but rose again to 3,492 U/L, and dysphagia recurred. Chest CT ruled out interstitial lung disease. Given the incomplete response, IVIG was restarted every 4 weeks for a planned 6 months. By the third cycle, CK had decreased to 202 U/L, dysphagia had resolved, strength had improved to 4/5, and she had regained assisted walking; rehabilitation is ongoing.

### Conclusión – Conclusion  (420 caracteres)

The anti-Mi-2 phenotype does not exclude a severe, polycyclic course. In this patient, adding IVIG in the setting of an incomplete glucocorticoid response was associated with clinical improvement on two occasions, and the monthly regimen, with a marked decrease in CK. In patients with prolonged steroid exposure and new weakness, elevated CK and MRI help distinguish inflammatory activity from steroid-induced myopathy.
