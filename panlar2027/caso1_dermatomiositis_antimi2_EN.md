**Title:**
Polycyclic anti-Mi-2 juvenile dermatomyositis with repeated response to intravenous immunoglobulin: a case report

**Topic:** Inflammatory Myopathies (Miopatías Inflamatorias)

**Keywords:** Dermatomyositis · Anti-Mi-2 antibodies · Intravenous immunoglobulin

---

### Antecedentes/Objetivo – Background/Objective  (476 caracteres)

Anti-Mi-2 juvenile dermatomyositis is usually associated with prominent muscle involvement, a low risk of interstitial lung disease and malignancy, and a good response to glucocorticoids, and is therefore considered a favorable phenotype. However, some patients follow a relapsing course that requires treatment escalation. Objective: to describe a patient with polycyclic anti-Mi-2 juvenile dermatomyositis and a repeated response to rescue intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG).

### Métodos – Methods  (156 caracteres)

Retrospective case report based on a review of the medical records, laboratory and immunological tests, muscle biopsy, and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI).

### Resultados – Results  (1514 caracteres)

A 17-year-old woman presented with Gottron's sign, skin lesions, myalgia, and progressive proximal weakness. One month later she had proximal strength of 3/5, dysphagia, creatine kinase (CK) of 12,588 U/L, ANA 1:160, and positive anti-Mi-2α/β antibodies; muscle biopsy showed inflammatory myopathy (2017 EULAR/ACR score: 9.6). She received methylprednisolone pulses, prednisolone, and azathioprine; because weakness and dysphagia persisted, IVIG 2 g/kg was added, with functional recovery and restored swallowing. Over the following 19 months she had four relapses, associated with mycophenolate withdrawal and glucocorticoid tapering (Table 1). Overlap with systemic lupus erythematosus was considered because of ANA 1:1280 and facial erythema, but was ruled out by negative anti-dsDNA and anti-RNP, normal complement, and absence of nephritis. At the fourth relapse, at age 19, she had 2/5 strength, inability to walk, and CK of 5,720 U/L. Given her cushingoid features, steroid-induced myopathy was considered; however, thigh MRI showed diffuse muscle edema with fascial involvement, consistent with inflammatory activity. After 5 days of methylprednisolone, CK fell to 2,073 U/L and then rose to 3,492 U/L, with new-onset dysphagia. Chest CT ruled out interstitial lung disease. IVIG was restarted every 4 weeks, planned for 6 months. By the third cycle, CK had normalized (XXX U/L), dysphagia had resolved, and strength had improved to 4/5; she was able to walk with assistance, and rehabilitation is ongoing.

### Conclusión – Conclusion  (352 caracteres)

The anti-Mi-2 phenotype does not exclude a severe, polycyclic course. In this patient, IVIG was effective on two occasions and achieved disease control despite an incomplete response to glucocorticoids. In patients with prolonged glucocorticoid exposure and new weakness, CK and MRI help distinguish inflammatory activity from steroid-induced myopathy.
